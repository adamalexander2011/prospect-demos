# Energy Systems II, Inc. — Flint, MI

**Status:** read on record, not built
**Their site:** energysystems2.com
**Score:** 40 plumbing + 22 design = **62** (both re-measured today, identical)
**Read written:** 27 August 2026

## The read

The `<h1>` on their homepage is:

> **"Learn More About Northstar Windows & Doors"**

The window manufacturer's name, in the one heading Google reads as the page's
subject. Directly beneath it sits a YouTube embed that renders, in plain text:

> **"Video can't be displayed"**

Broken. Under an `<h1>` that is not their name, pointing at a manufacturer, on a
page that has no other headline. The design probe reported "no headline element on
the page at all" because the only real one belongs to somebody else.

Now what is on the other pages.

**Nine customer letters, transcribed verbatim, mostly in capitals**, on
`/testimonials.html/`. These are not review-widget snippets. They are mailed
comment cards, and one of them says so: *"ALSO THIS COMMENT LETTER IS GREAT PR.
ALTHOUGH I HAD PEN IN HAND JUST AS IT ARRIVED."* Another: *"IF YOU NEED A
TESTIMONIAL, GIVE ME A CALL."* One is signed **"JUDGE M., FLINT, MI."**

Read them in a row and the same names keep coming back:

- **"RICK, GARY AND MATT ARE A FABULOUS BUNCH OF PEOPLE TO DO BUSINESS WITH... OUR
  YARD WAS CLEANED UP EVERY NIGHT."** (Mr. and Mrs. M, Grand Blanc)
- **"MIKE WAS VERY PROFESSIONAL... GARY AND SCOTT DID A FANTASTIC JOB."** (R.F., Flint)
- **"DAVE, GARY AND GLEN DID A GREAT JOB."** (R.D., Clio)
- **"YOUR INSTALLERS, GARY, SCOTT, GLEN AND TODD WORKED ON OUR HOME AS IF IT WAS
  THEIR OWN."** (J.B., Grand Blanc)

**Gary is named in four separate letters.** Scott and Glen in two each. And on the
Remodeling page, in capitals, is the claim those letters accidentally prove:

> **"WE DO NOT USE SUBCONTRACTORS WE USE OUR OWN EMPLOYEES."**

Every other contractor in Genesee County says a version of that sentence. This one
has nine unrelated customers, over what looks like years, naming the same four
installers. That is the asset, and it is on page three.

The rest of what they publish, all of it real and all of it buried:

- **36 years.** Family owned and operated, fully licensed and insured.
- **A showroom.** Mon-Thurs 10am-5:30pm, Fri 10am-5pm, evenings and weekends by
  appointment. Almost nobody in this trade has a room you can walk into.
- **"PROFESSIONALLY INSTALLED OR CASH AND CARRY"** and **"(Contractors Welcome)"**.
  You can buy windows over the counter from them. Nobody else on this board sells
  two ways.
- **North Star vinyl windows** with a **lifetime warranty**, **Quest vinyl siding
  and trim**, **Energy Star** products, acrylic bath liners in **3/8 inch material,
  "thickest in industry"**.
- **"SAVE UP TO $1500.00 ON YOUR WINDOW OR DOOR PROJECT"** via the energy tax
  credit. The only dollar figure published by any of the twelve.
- **8 exterior colors and 3 interior options.** Countable, specific, theirs.
- Tub-to-shower conversions, **handicapped baths**, Safety Tubs.
- 14 named towns. Address **G4084 Fenton Rd, Flint, MI 48507-3661**.

**And the gallery.** `/gallery.html/` carries **41 photographs**. Every one has
`alt=""`. Every one is served from a 32-character hash with no filename and no
extension. And the page contains **no text whatsoever** other than the footer
address and the cookie banner. Forty-one photographs of their own work, zero words
telling anyone what any of them is. I measured five: 359x480, 359x480, 292x400,
624x289, 640x480. Portrait phone shapes, small.

One customer wrote: **"THE BEFORE AND AFTER PICTURES ARE PHENOMENAL."** So
before-and-afters exist. They are not identifiable in that grid.

The site is five pages on the Web.com / Network Solutions builder, two of which live
at URLs called `1781624.html` and `1781625.html`. Draft.js editor attributes
(`contenteditable="false"`, `data-offset-key`) are serialized into the production
markup. Four of the homepage images are `.gif` files. Copyright 2026.

**Angle:** you say you never use subcontractors, and nine customer letters prove it by naming Gary, Scott and Glen over and over. Your homepage headline is the window manufacturer's name.

## Treatment

| Decision | Call |
|---|---|
| Photography | **41 photos, unlabeled and small** (roughly 300-640px, mostly portrait). Enough for a dense grid, not for a hero. **Ask Rick for the before-and-afters** a customer already described in writing, and for originals. Nothing here is stock, which puts them ahead of half this board. |
| Prices | **"Save up to $1500.00 on your window or door project"** is published and exact. Use it exactly, attributed to the energy tax credit the way they attribute it, and do not restate the credit's terms. No other figures. |
| Reviews | **Nine, verbatim, with the attributions exactly as given** (initials and town, "JUDGE M., FLINT, MI."). Keep the capitals or note that you set them in sentence case, but do not silently rewrite a customer's letter. Lead with the ones naming installers. |
| Credentials | **36 years**, family owned, licensed and insured, no subcontractors, North Star lifetime warranty, Quest siding, Energy Star, 3/8 inch acrylic, showroom hours, cash and carry, contractors welcome, 8 exterior and 3 interior options. All published by them. Do not invent a founding year from "36 years." |
| Structure | Crew first. Names, then the letters that name them, then the no-subcontractors line as the proof. Then the showroom and the hours, because a room you can walk into is the second-best thing they own. Windows, siding, baths as three blocks. The Northstar video gets fixed or dropped, not featured. |

**The Northstar `<h1>` is worth handling the way Buck & Bossman's Walters branding
was.** They may have been handed that headline by the manufacturer's marketing kit.
Do not open by telling them their headline is wrong. Show them what "Energy Systems
II, 36 years, Gary and Scott and Glen" looks like in that position, and let them
decide what to do about Northstar.

**One thing to check before writing:** the testimonials carry no dates. They read
old, and "Sterling Hts" is well outside the 14 towns they list. Ask roughly when
they are from before putting them on a page, and if the answer is uncomfortable,
say so in the disclosure rather than dropping them. They are still the best thing
this company has.
