What you’re actually buying

Six questions worth asking anyone who quotes you.

Concrete is one of the few things you buy where important work is buried before you ever see it. These are general planning prompts written for this concept, not claims about DeBoer’s methods or specifications.

01

What is going under it?

Base preparation and drainage can affect settlement and cracking. Soil, load and site conditions vary, so ask what preparation is proposed for your particular job.

02

How thick, actually?

Four inches and six inches are different prices and different slabs. A pad that will hold equipment, a hoist, or a loaded truck is not the same pour as a garden path. Get the number written down rather than implied.

03

Is there steel in it?

Rebar, mesh, fibres, or another specification may be proposed. Ask what reinforcement is included and why it suits the expected loads and design.

04

Where will it crack?

Concrete commonly cracks. Ask how joints will be laid out and when they will be formed or cut to encourage cracking at intended locations.

05

How is it finished and cured?

Finish and curing choices affect appearance, traction and performance. Ask what is specified for the job and what care is expected after placement.

06

Who is actually pouring it?

Concrete is unforgiving about timing. Once it is in the forms the clock runs whatever the weather does, and there is no pausing to think it over. Ask who will be on site and what happens if the pour goes sideways.

Ask all six. Then compare written scopes as well as bottom-line prices. Thickness, reinforcement, base preparation, joints, finish and curing should be discussed for the actual site rather than inferred from this concept.
