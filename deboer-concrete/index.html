What you’re actually buying

Six questions worth asking anyone who quotes you.

Concrete is one of the few things you buy where the expensive part is buried before you ever see it. Two quotes can look identical on paper and be completely different slabs. These are the questions that tell them apart.

01

What is going under it?

A slab is only as good as what it sits on. Undisturbed or properly compacted base, graded to drain, is what stops a slab settling and cracking in the first two winters. If nobody mentions the base, ask why.

02

How thick, actually?

Four inches and six inches are different prices and different slabs. A pad that will hold equipment, a hoist, or a loaded truck is not the same pour as a garden path. Get the number written down rather than implied.

03

Is there steel in it?

Rebar, mesh, or nothing at all. It changes what happens after the slab cracks, which matters because concrete does crack. Reinforcement is what keeps a crack from becoming two separate slabs at different heights.

04

Where will it crack?

Honest answer: everywhere eventually. Control joints are how you decide in advance where it happens, cut straight and in the right places so the cracking goes where you put it instead of across the middle of your floor.

05

How is it finished and cured?

Broom, trowel, or exposed changes how it wears and how it handles ice. Curing is the boring part that decides the surface strength, and it is the easiest corner to cut because nobody is standing there watching.

06

Who is actually pouring it?

Concrete is unforgiving about timing. Once it is in the forms the clock runs whatever the weather does, and there is no pausing to think it over. Ask who will be on site and what happens if the pour goes sideways.

Ask us all six. Then ask whoever else is quoting you the same six, and compare the answers rather than the bottom line. If a quote is cheaper because it is thinner, has no steel in it, or skips the base, that is worth knowing before it is in the ground and not after.
